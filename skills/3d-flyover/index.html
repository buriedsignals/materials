/ Fit

What it’s for

Reach for it when

  • The story needs a cinematic reveal of a real place—fly through a canyon, over a city or mountain range, or along a border or supply route.
  • You want true 3D geometry with sharp ridgelines or recognizable architecture, not a flat map tilted back.
  • The shot must be reproducible: re-render after a script change and get the identical move.

Use another skill when

  • A flat “where is this?” map communicates the geography more clearly.
  • The evidence is primarily numerical and belongs in a chart.
  • A Google city render cannot meet its current usage conditions.

/ Modes

Choose the world the story needs

City mode

Google Photorealistic 3D Tiles

Use for cities, architecture, and recognizable landmarks. Set a billing-enabled Google Map Tiles API key and keep provider attribution visible.

Landscape mode

MapTiler terrain + satellite

Use quantized-mesh terrain and satellite imagery for mountains, gorges, rivers, coastlines, and rural routes.

City-render constraint

Google’s current Map Tiles policy limits promotional application videos to 30 seconds and requires the “For promotional purposes only” marker. The component enforces both conditions.

/ Architecture

Three parts, one job each

Cesium draws the world, the selected tile provider feeds it, and Remotion runs the clock.

Draws the world

CesiumJS

A true free camera in a 3D globe that renders to the horizon, driven one frame at a time with its default render loop switched off.

Feeds the world

Google or MapTiler

Photorealistic 3D Tiles supply cities. Quantized-mesh terrain plus draped satellite imagery supplies landscapes.

Runs the clock

Remotion

Steps the timeline frame by frame and holds each frame until the required streamed tiles are settled before capture.

/ Pipeline

From a line on a map to a finished shot

Prepare the camera path

Start from a route centerline or sparse control points. For landscapes, the prep script clips, resamples, smooths, and dampens the line. The component then applies repeated curve smoothing.

Fly the path

The camera walks the curve by arc length and aims at a real point farther along it. The rate of turn drives a subtle roll, so the camera banks into bends instead of twitching left and right.

Settle and render

Remotion waits for the globe or tileset to load, calls viewer.render(), and captures deterministic frames through headless Chrome with --gl=angle.

The rendering gotcha this skill exists to spare you

Disable Cesium’s own render loop, drive every frame with viewer.render()—never scene.render()—and gate initialization and capture with delayRender until the relevant tiles are loaded.
